Sleep Is a Foundation, Not a Luxury

Why sleep matters and why persistent problems deserve attention.

Sleep is active biology

Sleep is not simply time when the body switches off. During sleep, the brain cycles through distinct states, and the ability to pay attention, learn, remember, and manage emotion is affected by whether sleep is sufficient and restorative. Sleep is one part of a larger picture that includes medical conditions, daily routines, stress, activity, and the sleeping environment.

Quantity and quality both matter

Time in bed is not identical to time asleep. A person can spend eight hours in bed and still have fragmented sleep. CDC guidance lists seven or more hours for adults ages 18–60, seven to nine hours for ages 61–64, and seven to eight hours for adults 65 and older. These are general recommendations, not a personal prescription. See the CDC source in the references.

Observe before you guess

For one or two weeks, record bedtime, estimated time to fall asleep, awakenings, final wake time, naps, caffeine, alcohol, and daytime alertness. A simple diary is often more useful than trying to interpret every sleep-stage estimate from a consumer device. Sleep difficulties are a signal to understand, not proof that a particular supplement is needed.
