The Two Forces That Shape Sleep

Understand sleep pressure and the circadian clock, and why timing matters.

Sleep pressure builds while you are awake

Your need for sleep increases the longer you remain awake. This is sometimes called sleep pressure or sleep drive. A long nap late in the day can reduce some of the pressure that had been building and make bedtime harder for some people.

The circadian clock helps set the schedule

Your body also uses an internal timing system that runs on an approximately 24-hour cycle. Light and darkness are important cues. Morning light generally supports daytime alertness and helps anchor timing; bright light late at night can make it harder to feel sleepy at the intended time. NHLBI explains how the sleep-wake cycle responds to light, darkness, and other cues.

The systems work together

A person can feel exhausted but still be alert at an unusual biological hour. Regular wake times, morning daylight, and a calmer evening routine can help create more predictable cues. Claims that a supplement can simply reset a circadian clock should be treated carefully: product-specific claims require evidence on the finished product, not just a proposed mechanism for one ingredient.
